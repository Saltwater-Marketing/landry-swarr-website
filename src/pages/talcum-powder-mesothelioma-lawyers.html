Can Talcum Powder Cause Mesothelioma?

Talc and asbestos are different naturally occurring minerals. However, they can be found near one another in the earth, creating the potential for talc to become contaminated with asbestos during mining or processing.

Asbestos exposure is a recognized cause of mesothelioma, a serious cancer that may develop decades after exposure.

Not all talcum powder contains asbestos. However, allegations involving asbestos-contaminated talc have led to lawsuits against manufacturers of certain baby powders and cosmetic products.

For someone diagnosed with mesothelioma, a history of using talc-containing products may be an important part of investigating potential asbestos exposure.

A legal investigation may consider which products were used, how frequently they were used, when the exposure occurred, and what evidence exists concerning asbestos contamination.

Johnson & Johnson Talcum Powder Litigation in Louisiana

Landry & Swarr has been directly involved in litigation concerning Johnson & Johnson baby powder and mesothelioma.

In Roy v. Johnson & Johnson, Landry & Swarr partner Matthew C. Clark helped represent the family of Lynne Roy, a New Orleans-area woman who died from mesothelioma in 2020.

The case involved allegations that Ms. Roy’s lifelong use of Johnson & Johnson baby powder exposed her to asbestos and contributed to her disease.

Following a three-week trial in Orleans Parish, the jury found that Ms. Roy had significant exposure to asbestos in a product manufactured by Johnson & Johnson and that the exposure was a cause of her mesothelioma.

The jury awarded $10 million in total damages, with Johnson & Johnson held responsible for approximately $2 million and the remainder attributed to other parties.

The Roy verdict demonstrates the importance of carefully investigating potential asbestos exposure from consumer products, even when other possible exposure sources exist.

Who May Have a Talcum Powder Mesothelioma Claim?

Individuals diagnosed with mesothelioma who have a history of using talc-containing products may have questions about whether a legal claim is available.

Potential cases may involve people who regularly used baby powder, body powder, or talc-containing cosmetics over many years.

In some situations, surviving family members may also have legal options after losing a loved one to mesothelioma.

However, using talcum powder or receiving a mesothelioma diagnosis does not automatically establish that a particular product caused the illness or that a legal claim exists.

Each case requires an evaluation of the available evidence, potential exposure sources, and applicable Louisiana law.

You do not need to know exactly where or when asbestos exposure occurred before contacting our firm. Our attorneys can help evaluate the circumstances and determine what additional investigation may be appropriate.
