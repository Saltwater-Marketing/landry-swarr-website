Louisiana is located along the Intracoastal Waterway, which is why there are so many companies in maritime industries in the New Orleans area. It is also one of the main reasons there are so many asbestos cases in Louisiana related to shipyards.

Avondale Shipyard was a Louisiana-based shipbuilding company that was ultimately acquired by the Northrop Grumman Corporation. The company builds and maintains a wide variety of commercial surface ships including vessels for the United States Navy and Coast Guard.

Since Avondale Industries was founded seven decades ago, more than 2,500 ships have been produced in their facilities. The variety of ships produced by the company include drilling barges, work boats, tankers, offshore drilling rigs, guided missile destroyers, and more. Many of these ships played an important role in the efforts of the U.S. military in World War II. With 6,000 workers and a 268-acre facility, the Avondale Operations Division is currently the largest Louisiana-based industrial manufacturing company.

Where Exposure May Have Occurred

Prior to 1980, workers at the Avondale facility may have potentially been exposed to asbestos, a material that was commonly used for a variety of purposes throughout the ship building industry since it worked as an effective flame retardant. Asbestos was used in many parts of the ship to control heat, and workers may have come in contact with this material on everything from boilers to pipes and even insulation. Workers may also have been exposed to it by simply passing through a section of the ship where the material was being utilized.

Employers such as Avondale which includes company owners and executives, have known about the dangers of asbestos since at least the early 1940s. However, the workers were not informed about the dangers until well into the 1970s or 1980s.
